The Fluency Trap — Why Polish Stopped Meaning What It Used to Mean

For centuries, polished writing was a cheap and usually-correct proxy for the mind behind it — because polish was expensive, and its cost fell hardest on exactly the people who had the least to say. This episode takes that heuristic apart: the processing-fluency psychology that makes ease feel like truth, the Spence signaling economics that kept the proxy honest, the measured collapse once machines made polish free — experts at coin-flip accuracy, machine essays out-scoring students, detectors false-flagging non-native and neurodivergent writers — and what still works when fluency doesn't: process evidence, live elaboration, and rubrics that pay polish nothing.

Partner episode to "Knowing What's True in the Age of AI."
