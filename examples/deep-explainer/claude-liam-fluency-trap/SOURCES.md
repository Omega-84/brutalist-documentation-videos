## Corrections / editorial decisions (DOUBLE-CHECK LAW)

1. **Real-person care (B22).** The court case is narrated WITHOUT the
   student's name; "Newby v. Adelphi Univ., 2026" appears only as a small
   on-screen citation card and here. The episode's point is the detector
   and the institution, never the student. Detector brand names (Turnitin,
   Grammarly, ZeroGPT) are NOT spoken — "one detector … two others" — to
   keep the beat mechanism-first and less datable; brands live here for the
   record.
2. **The wrong-attribution variant (B23).** The source documents a
   circulating version of the case with a different student/school/year.
   The episode teaches the cascade WITHOUT repeating the wrong name on
   screen or in narration — repeating a misattribution amplifies it, which
   is the failure the beat is about. The wrong version's details stay in
   SOURCE.md only.
3. **Numbers policy.** Figures spoken or shown ($26, −19%/+14%, 49.9%,
   56%, 94%, 61.3%) are all from the source's cited studies and carry
   on-screen citations. The 65–80% grader-agreement and Turnitin-claim
   figures were NOT used (one more stat than the acts needed). Nothing
   else quantified is invented; schematic graphics (Spence curves, ease
   dials) carry no fabricated units.
4. **Datable material trimmed.** University names that paused detectors
   ("several major universities" spoken; names here: Vanderbilt, Michigan
   State, Northwestern), the Australian-university case count, and vendor
   names — all cut or generalized in narration per deep-explainer hard
   rule 4.
5. **Landy & Sigall (B09).** Narrated as "the classic demonstrations"
   without on-screen invented scores; the study manipulated essay quality
   AND other surface cues — the beat's claim stays at the source's level
   (writing quality itself shifted competence judgments).
