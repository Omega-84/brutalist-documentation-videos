The Dashboard That Lied — Seeing Is Not Doing

From Chapter 1 of Bear's Living Models book. A dashboard shows 18 percent growth and none of it is real — not because anyone lied, but because a silent refresh failure shrank the denominator and the instrument drifted in the direction that felt like good news. Then the same failure at catastrophic scale: J.C. Penney's numbers were true, the inference was wrong, and $4.3 billion followed it down. The gap between them has a name — P(Y|X) versus P(Y|do(X)), Judea Pearl's do-operator — and a shape: Pearl's Ladder, where no rung is reachable by piling up more data on the rung below. The episode closes with what the book is building toward: Living Models — causal, counterfactual, continually updated, treatment-oriented — and observability, the instrument that verifies itself.
